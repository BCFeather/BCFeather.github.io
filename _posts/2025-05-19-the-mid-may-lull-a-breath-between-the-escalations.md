The middle of May brought a pause in the year's conflicts that the calendars had not scheduled, and the pause was the kind that the coverage has no word for because it is the absence of the news rather than the news itself. The tariff announcements were spaced out through the fortnight, the negotiations continued without the crises that the spring had produced, and the markets settled into the trading ranges that the absence of the headlines allowed; the lull was the counterpoint to the year's pace, and the counterpoint was worth a moment's notice, because the lulls are where the strategies get adjusted, the positions get rebuilt and the next round gets prepared.

The markets' behaviour in the lull was the part of the fortnight that the analysts found most instructive. The indexes drifted through the ranges that the spring's swings had established, the volatility measures fell from the peaks that the tariff announcements had produced, and the trading volumes declined with the attention that the markets pay when the catalysts are absent; the drift was the market's way of consolidating the gains and the losses that the spring had delivered, and the consolidation is the work that the markets do when the headlines are quiet. The lull's market was the market at work, and the work was the preparation for the events that the summer will bring.

The negotiations' quiet progress through the lull was the part that the diplomats described with the particular caution of the profession. The technical sessions continued, the draft texts were exchanged and revised, and the officials' statements, issued at the intervals that the process requires, described the talks in the language of the steady advance; the progress was the kind that the headlines cannot carry, the carve-outs, the definitions, the mechanisms, the details that the agreements are made of. The lull's negotiations were the negotiations at their most productive, and the productivity was the counterpart of the quiet that the markets were enjoying.

The politics of the lull were the part that the commentators could not quite trust, because the year's pattern had taught the world to expect the next escalation. The leaders' statements were measured, the deadlines that the spring had created had been pushed or paused, and the domestic pressures that had driven the escalations had found their outlets in the other stories of the fortnight; the quiet was real, and the realness was the part that the markets and the analysts had to weigh against the memory of the spring. The lull's politics were the politics of the breath, and the breath was being taken by both sides, which is the condition that the trade wars require before the settlements.

The lull's end will come, as the lulls always end, with the event that the calendar or the politics will provide: the deadline, the announcement, the crisis that the summer's schedule has been preparing. The preparation is the lull's meaning, and the meaning is the reason the pause was worth the notice: the mid-May quiet was not the end of the year's conflicts but the interval between their rounds, the space where the strategies were adjusted and the positions rebuilt. The markets drifted, the negotiators worked, and the world took the breath that the spring had earned; the breath was brief, and the summer's rounds will arrive with the certainty that the year's calendar has established, but the lull itself, quiet and productive, was the fortnight that the year's pace had not scheduled and the year's work had made necessary.
