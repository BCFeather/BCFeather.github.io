The administration announced a ninety-day pause in the application of the broad tariffs in the second week of April, and the markets responded with the largest rally in years, the indexes recovering, in a single session, the losses that the original announcement had produced a week earlier. The pause was announced with the particulars of the conflict written into it, the reduced rate for the partners that had not retaliated, the increased rate for the partner that had, and the message that the pause was not a retreat from the policy but a stage of the negotiation; the markets took the pause as a gift, and the celebration was the acknowledgement that the pause was also the first visible flexibility that the policy had shown.

The details of the pause are the part that the coverage has parsed with the care that the market's reaction earned. The pause applied to the broad programme for ninety days, with the universal rate reduced to a level that the analysts described as the background tariff, and the specific rates for the largest partner raised, in the administration's language, as the consequence of the retaliation; the structure of the pause, the analysts noted, was the administration's way of rewarding the partners that accepted the terms and punishing the one that resisted, and the structure was the negotiation's first explicit move. The markets read the pause as the signal that the policy would be applied with the flexibility that the negotiations required, and the signal was the day's real content.

The equity market's reaction to the pause was the demonstration of the damage that the week had done and the relief that the pause provided. The indexes rallied through the session, the technology stocks leading the recovery with the particular speed that their sector's repricing demanded, and the closing levels, published in the green, recovering the ground that the markets had lost since the original announcement; the volatility measures fell from the peaks that the week had produced, and the trading desks described the session as the release of the pressure that the pause had made safe to release. The rally was the market's verdict on the pause, and the verdict was the judgement that the trade conflict's first round had ended with the negotiators back at the table.

The bond market's reaction to the pause was the quieter signal, and the signal was the one that the economists will study through the year. The yields moved with the inflation expectations that the pause revised, the longer maturities falling as the markets priced the reduced likelihood of the full tariff programme, and the central bank's path, the futures showed, was being repriced around the lower inflation risk; the bond market's message was the one that the equity rally obscured: the pause had reduced the probability of the worst outcome, and the reduction was worth more than the day's gains. The economic effects of the pause will be felt through the quarter, and the effects will be the subject of the models that the pause's announcement made necessary.

The politics of the pause are the part that the next ninety days will test. The administration has framed the pause as the instrument of the negotiation, the period in which the partners can offer the concessions that the full programme would extract, and the partners have framed their responses as the defence of their interests within the window that the pause provides; the negotiation will run through the summer, with the deadline of the pause's end providing the calendar, and the outcome will be determined by the concessions that the two sides can offer and accept. The pause was the trade conflict's first major inflection, and the inflection's meaning will be decided by the talks that the pause has made possible; the markets took the pause as a gift, and the gift's value will be measured by the use that the negotiators make of the ninety days.
