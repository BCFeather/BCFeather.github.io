The first half of 2025 closed at the end of June, and the closing brought the particular opportunity that the half-year's ends provide: the chance to read the year's events as a whole, to see the shape that the six months have taken. The record is the one that the coverage has assembled piece by piece, the tariff conflicts and the truces, the elections and the transitions, the conclave and the new pontificate, the wars that will not settle and the negotiations that the summer will carry; the record's shape, read from the distance of the closing week, is the attempt that this essay makes, and the attempt is worth making because the second half will be conducted in the presence of the first.

The trade conflict has been the year's dominant story, and the story's arc through the half-year has been the arc of the escalation and the adjustment. The tariffs arrived with the Liberation Day announcement, the markets delivered their arithmetic, the pauses were declared and the truces were tested, the first agreements were signed and the largest talks reached their decisive stages; the half-year's trade story is the story of the doctrine applied and the costs measured, and the second half will be the story of the settlements that the doctrine either produces or fails to produce. The conflict's shape is the year's shape, and the shape's most striking feature is the speed with which the world has adjusted to it.

The politics of the half-year carried the transitions that the crises had set in motion. The German election produced the government that the coalition talks assembled, the Korean election turned the page that the martial-law night had opened, and the church's conclave chose the pope that the late pontiff's appointments had prepared; the transitions were conducted through the institutions, and the institutions' performance was the half-year's quietest reassurance. The political record is the record of the systems working, and the working is the part that the coverage of the crises tends to miss.

The wars and the security questions that the half-year could not settle will be carried into the second half with the weight that their persistence has accumulated. The war on the continent's edge continued through the negotiations that its diplomacy produced, the rearmament that it forced moved from the commitments to the procurement, and the nuclear file returned to the table with the new cast that the year had produced; the security record is the record of the efforts, and the efforts' outcomes will be measured in the second half. The wars will not settle because the wars' terms have not moved, and the terms' movement is the work that the summer will have to do.

The second half of 2025 is already scheduled: the trade deadlines, the summits, the sessions, the campaigns that the year's calendar has fixed. The schedule is the year's continuation, and the continuation will be conducted in the presence of the first half's lessons: the costs of the conflicts are real and measurable, the institutions are holding, and the adjustments that the year has demanded are being made at the pace that the events allow. June closes with the half-year in the rear mirror, and the mirror's image is the year's shape as the events have drawn it: the doctrine applied, the costs measured, the transitions conducted, the settlements pending. The second half will write the ending, and the ending's shape, on the evidence of the first, will be determined by the settlements that the summer produces and the adjustments that the autumn will require.
