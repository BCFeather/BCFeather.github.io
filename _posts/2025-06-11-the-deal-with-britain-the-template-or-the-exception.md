The first trade agreement of the new tariff era was signed in the second week of June, with the two governments announcing the deal that the negotiations had produced and the cameras capturing the signing that the year's conflicts had made notable. The agreement covered the sectors that the negotiations had mapped, the machinery, the services, the agriculture, and it carried the reductions that the tariff programme had made available to the partners that accepted the terms; the signing was conducted with the particular ceremony that the occasion's rarity merited, and the coverage, in the days after, asked the question that the deal invites: whether the agreement is the template for the year's other negotiations or the exception that the special relationship allowed. The answer will be written by the talks that follow.

The deal's terms are the part that the analysts have weighed against the year's tariff arithmetic. The agreement reduced the tariffs on the sectors that the two economies trade most, it secured the access that the exporting industries had sought, and it left the sensitive sectors, the agriculture, the automobiles, subject to the arrangements that the negotiations could not resolve; the terms are the compromise that the two sides' positions produced, and the compromise is the reason the deal could be signed while the larger negotiations remain open. The deal's economics are real and contained, and the containment is the part that the analysts will be watching as the template question is tested.

The politics of the signing were the part of the week that the two governments celebrated with the particular care that the year's audience requires. The American administration presented the deal as the demonstration of the new trade doctrine, the proof that the tariffs extract the deals that the old system would not have produced; the British government presented the deal as the management of the relationship with the largest trading partner, the securing of the access that the post-Brexit economy needs; the two presentations are compatible, and the compatibility is the deal's political value. The signing's coverage carried the relief of the two governments, and the relief was the measure of the year's pressures.

The template question is the one that the year's other negotiations are now testing. The partners whose talks remain open, the European Union, the Asian economies, the neighbours, will read the British deal for the signals about the terms that the administration will accept, and the reading will shape their own negotiations; the deal's carve-outs and its sectors will provide the reference points, and the reference points will be the starting positions of the talks that the summer will conduct. The British agreement is the first data point of the tariff era, and the data points that follow will determine whether the first one was the template or the exception.

The year's trade story, whatever the template question's answer, has been changed by the signing. The deal demonstrated that the tariff programme can produce agreements, that the pauses can be converted into deals, and that the partners can find the terms that both sides' politics can accept; the demonstration is the hope that the year's other negotiations will now be conducted under. The deal with Britain will be followed by the talks with the other partners, and the talks will be conducted with the British agreement as the reference, the terms compared, the sectors weighed, the compromises tested; the template or the exception, the first agreement of the tariff era has set the standard that the year's remaining negotiations will now have to meet, and the meeting will be the trade story's next chapter.
