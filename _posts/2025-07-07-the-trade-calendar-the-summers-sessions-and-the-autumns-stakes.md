The trade negotiations that the year has conducted moved through the first week of July with the calendar that the summer has set, and the calendar's shape was the one that the spring's events had produced. The European-American talks were at their decisive stage, with the draft texts circulating and the leaders' calls scheduled; the consultations with the other partners, the Asian economies, the neighbours, the Gulf states, were continuing at the pace that their files require; and the autumn's deadlines, the dates that the pauses and the programmes have fixed, were already casting their shadows over the negotiators' planning. The summer's sessions will determine whether the tariff era produces the settlements that its doctrine promises or the deadlocks that its costs threaten, and the determining is the work of the weeks that the calendar has scheduled.

The European-American talks are the file that the calendar's first weeks will decide, and the file's state, as the week opened, was the state of the negotiations within reach. The sectors have been named, the texts have been drafted, and the terms that remain, the rates, the access, the mechanisms, have been narrowed to the questions that the leaders will have to settle; the sessions of the coming weeks will conduct the narrowing, and the leaders' calls will provide the moments of the decision. The talks' outcome will be the year's largest trade settlement, and the settlement's template will shape the negotiations that follow; the stakes are the reason the calendar's first weeks carry so much weight.

The other files that the calendar schedules are the ones that the coverage tracks with less attention and the negotiations conduct with the same care. The consultations with the partners whose deals are smaller, the exemptions that the sectors have sought, the disputes that the mechanisms are testing, all of it is being processed through the channels that the year has built; the smaller files are the accumulation that the tariff era's settlements will be made of, and the accumulation is the negotiators' quiet work. The summer's calendar holds the sessions that the files require, and the sessions' outcomes will be the sum that the year's trade record will carry.

The autumn's deadlines are the part of the calendar that gives the summer its urgency. The dates that the pauses and the programmes have fixed, the moments when the suspensions would lapse and the rates would return, are the leverage that both sides hold and the pressure that both sides feel; the deadlines concentrate the negotiators' minds, and the concentration is the mechanism by which the trade conflicts move. The summer's sessions are being conducted against the deadlines, and the deadlines' approach will accelerate the work that the sessions have been doing.

The summer's trade story will be written by the sessions that the calendar holds, and the writing will be the test of the year's doctrine. The settlements that the sessions produce will demonstrate that the tariffs extract the deals that the doctrine promises; the deadlocks that the sessions cannot resolve will demonstrate the costs that the doctrine imposes; and the markets, the industries and the voters will read the results with the attention that the stakes command. The trade calendar that the summer has set is the schedule of the year's largest economic decisions, and the schedule's outcomes will be determined by the negotiations that the weeks ahead will conduct; the sessions are scheduled, the stakes are set, and the settlements, or the deadlocks, are the product that the summer will deliver.
