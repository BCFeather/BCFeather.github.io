The country's birthday arrived on a Friday in 2025, and the holiday was observed, across the time zones, with the rituals that the day has accumulated over two hundred and forty-nine years: the parades in the small towns, the fireworks over the cities, the cookouts in the backyards, and the speeches that the occasion requires from the politicians and the commentators. The birthday fell in the middle of the year's argument about the country's direction, and the argument's presence was the holiday's particular character this year: the celebration was conducted against the background of the trade conflicts, the institutional tensions and the divisions that the year has made visible, and the background shaped the way the day was kept.

The rituals themselves were the part that the coverage leads with, and the rituals' continuity is the day's real content. The parades that the towns organize, the flags that the porches display, the fireworks that the cities budget for, the national anthem that the stadiums play, all of it was performed with the regularity that the tradition commands; the continuity is the point, the demonstration that the republic's habits survive the arguments that its politics conduct. The day's rituals are the country's way of rehearsing its own existence, and the rehearsal is the reassurance that the year's crises have made more valuable.

The speeches that the day produced carried the year's argument into the holiday's language. The president's address, delivered from the ceremony that the White House stages, rehearsed the themes that the year has established, the strength, the sovereignty, the deal-making that the doctrine celebrates; the responses, from the opposition and the commentators, carried the counter-themes, the institutions, the alliances, the values that the year's policies have tested. The two registers were both present in the day's words, and their presence was the holiday's honest reflection of the country's condition: the republic that celebrates its birthday is also the republic that argues about what the birthday means.

The holiday's economics were the part that the retailers and the travel industry measured with the care that their businesses require. The long weekend moved the millions of travellers that the calendars had predicted, the cookouts and the fireworks supplied the industries that the day supports, and the spending, reported in the estimates that the analysts produce, held the levels that the year's inflation and the trade costs might have depressed; the day's commerce was the demonstration of the economy's resilience, and the resilience was the counterpoint to the conflicts that the year has conducted. The Fourth is the country's largest secular holiday, and the largeness is measured in the receipts as well as the rituals.

The birthday's meaning, in the year of the argument, is the part that the commentators will keep weighing through the summer. The country that celebrates its foundation is also the country that has spent the year renegotiating its relationships, its alliances, its trade, its institutions; the celebration and the renegotiation are both expressions of the same energy, the same confidence, the same insistence on the country's terms. The Fourth of July at two hundred and forty-nine was observed with the rituals intact and the argument present, and the combination is the country's condition: the republic that knows how to celebrate itself, and the republic that is still arguing about what it wants to be. The fireworks faded over the cities, the parades returned to the garages, and the argument resumed on the Monday, as the year's business requires; the birthday had been kept, and the keeping is the part that the argument cannot take away.
