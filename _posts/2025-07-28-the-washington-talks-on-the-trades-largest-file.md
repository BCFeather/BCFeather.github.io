The negotiations between the world's two largest economies resumed in Washington at the end of July, and the resumption brought the delegations together for the sessions that the truce's expiration had made necessary and the summer's diplomacy had been preparing. The talks, conducted in the capital that the year's trade conflicts have centred on, opened with the positions that the spring's escalations had hardened and the expectations that the summer's other settlements had adjusted; the two countries' trade relationship is the largest file of the tariff era, and the file's outcome will shape the year's remaining trade politics and the global economy that the two countries' trade anchors.

The context of the talks is the part that explains their timing and their stakes. The truce that the spring had declared expired without the settlement, the tariffs returned on schedule, and the costs that the spring had measured have accumulated through the summer's months; the other settlements, the British and the European deals, have provided the template that the largest file is now being measured against, and the template has raised the expectations that the Washington sessions will have to meet. The talks are the trade conflict's test case, and the test's preparation has been conducted through the channels that the year has maintained.

The positions that the delegations carried into the sessions are the ones that the year's events have established. The American negotiators have pressed the reductions in the barriers, the market access and the structural changes that the administration's doctrine requires, with the tariffs as the leverage and the settlements as the incentive; the Chinese negotiators have insisted on the removal of the tariffs that the escalation imposed, the respect for the trading system, and the terms that the domestic politics can accept; the two positions have been mapped through the previous rounds, and the mapping has left the political terms to be tested in the sessions that the week will hold.

The markets' attention to the talks has been the particular register of the file's importance. The currencies and the bonds have moved with the reports from the sessions, the analysts have issued the notes on the scenarios that the talks could produce, and the trading desks have positioned for the outcomes with the care that the stakes command; the markets' attention is the recognition that the two countries' trade relationship is the global economy's largest variable, and the variable's resolution will be priced through the year. The sessions' reports will be the market's guide, and the guide's reliability will be tested by the talks' progress.

The talks' outcome will be determined by the sessions' course and the politics that the two governments bring to them, and the determining will be conducted through the week and the follow-ups that the negotiators will schedule. The settlement that the talks could produce would be the tariff era's largest, the completion of the template that the other deals have established; the deadlock that the talks could produce would return the conflict to the escalation that the year has measured. The Washington talks on the trade's largest file opened with the positions set and the expectations adjusted, and the sessions' outcome will be the year's trade story in its most consequential form; the delegations are at the table, and the table's product will be the settlement or the stalemate that the year's second half will live with.
