The final week of the Korean presidential campaign opened with the polls showing the race that the spring had shaped: the opposition's candidate ahead by the margin that the surveys have measured through the past weeks, the ruling party's candidate within reach of the lead, and the undecided voters, the portion of the electorate that will decide the outcome, preparing to make the decision that the first days of June will record. The campaign has run its course through the spring, the debates conducted, the rallies staged, the attacks traded, and the course has left the race in the state that the final week will settle; the election is days away, and the country's answer to the winter's question is about to be delivered.

The final week's calendar is the part that the campaigns have been managing with the particular care that the short races require. The last debates were scheduled for the week's middle, the rallies and the appearances will fill the remaining days, and the polls will be published and parsed with the attention that the closing stages always command; the events of the week will provide the moments that can move the race, and the campaigns' discipline will determine whether the moments are seized or suffered. The final week is the compressed version of the campaign, and the compression is the test that the two organizations will have to pass.

The issues that the final week will weigh are the ones that the spring has established, and the weighing will be conducted in the full presence of the winter's memory. The economy, the trade conflict, the security, all of it will be addressed through the campaign's final performances, and the martial-law night will provide the background against which the performances will be judged; the candidates' closing arguments, the messages that they will repeat through the final days, will be the distillations of the campaigns that the spring has built, and the distillations will be the material that the undecided voters use. The issues are the year's issues, and the year's issues are the ones that the night raised.

The uncertainty of the final week is the part that the analysts have been refusing to resolve, and the refusal is the honest position. The polls' margins are within the range that the surveys' errors can reverse, the undecided voters' movements are the unknown that the final events will settle, and the turnout, the factor that the Korean elections have taught the psephologists to watch, will be the final variable; the forecasts have been hedged, and the hedging is the recognition that the short campaigns' endings are the moments when the races can still turn. The final week's events will decide, and the deciding is the suspense that the election's closing always carries.

The vote on the first days of June will close the chapter that the martial-law night opened, and the closing will be the beginning of the country's next argument. The winner will govern through the year's challenges with the mandate that the margin will measure, and the loser will lead the opposition through the term that follows; the country will continue its argument about the night, the removal and the direction, and the argument will be conducted through the institutions that the crisis has tested and the election will confirm. The last week before Korea votes is the week of the closing arguments, and the closing will be followed by the opening that every election brings: the country will vote, the country will count, and the country will begin the work that the winter's question and the spring's campaign have prepared it for.
