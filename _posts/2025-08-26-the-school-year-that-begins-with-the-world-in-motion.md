The classrooms across the northern hemisphere began their new year at the end of August, and the beginning carried the particular combination that the season always brings: the fresh notebooks, the new routines, the uniforms and the backpacks, and the world's news arriving with the students through the doors. The school year is the calendar's most reliable rhythm, the rhythm that the wars and the markets and the politics cannot break, and the rhythm's return is worth a moment's notice in a year of change; the beginning is the annual demonstration that the world's work continues through the world's events.

The rituals of the beginning are the ones that the season has performed for generations. The classrooms were set, the schedules were distributed, the first lessons were delivered, and the particular silence of the first day, the silence of the students sizing up the rooms and the teachers, was broken by the routines that the year would follow; the rituals are the continuity that the institutions provide, and the continuity is the reassurance that the year's events cannot supply. The beginning was conducted with the regularity that the calendars have fixed, and the regularity is the season's content.

The new year's students are the part of the beginning that the coverage rarely reaches. The first-years who entered the schools and the universities, the graduates who left them, the teachers who returned and the ones who did not, all of them carried the particular hopes and anxieties that the beginnings produce, and the carrying is the school year's human substance; the students' world, the one they are being educated for, is the world that the news describes, and the description is the context of the education. The year's events, the trade conflicts, the wars, the climate, will be studied in the classrooms of the coming year, and the studying is the education's engagement with the world.

The year's education politics, meanwhile, continued through the summer's recesses and the autumn's openings. The debates about the curricula, the funding, the admissions, the technology, all of it resumed with the sessions that the autumn will hold, and the debates are the politics that the institutions conduct alongside their teaching; the debates' outcomes will shape the classrooms that the coming years will fill, and the shaping is the education's long story. The school year's opening is the annual intersection of the education's routines and its politics.

The year that the classrooms have begun will be conducted in the presence of the world's motion, and the motion is the context that the education will have to prepare its students for; the trade conflicts and the wars will be studied, the sciences and the technologies will be taught, and the skills that the students will need for the world they are entering will be the curriculum's hidden subject. The school year began at the end of August with the fresh notebooks and the world's news, and the combination is the season's particular gift: the rhythm that the year's events cannot break, and the preparation that the rhythm provides. The classrooms are full again, the routines have resumed, and the world's motion has been met, as it always is, by the world's most reliable institution, which is the school.
