The days between the holiday and the new year arrived with the particular quiet that the calendar reserves for them, and the quiet was the year's last. The year's business paused with the offices thinning and the schedules cleared, the streets carried the season's light with the particular calm that the week provides, and the year's record waited, in the last quiet, for the turning that the new year will bring; the days between are the year's threshold, and the threshold's keeping is the pause before the new year. The quiet was held with the particular acceptance that the season's end commands, and the holding is the week's content.

The year's pause is the part of the week that the calendars have set, and the pause's depth is the measure of the year's weight. The sessions and the deadlines that the year has conducted have given way to the week's calm, the follow-ups scheduled for the new year and the offices running at the reduced rhythms that the season imposes; the pause is the year's breath, and the breath's taking is the week's gift. The year's business will resume, and the resumption is the reason the pause is only the threshold.

The week's light is the part of the days that the season's end provides, and the light's holding is the winter's quiet theatre: the early dusks, the decorations that the cities keep, and the particular glow that the turning's approach carries. The light is the season's staging, and the staging is the week's atmosphere; the days are dressed for the turning, and the dressing is the week's quiet work.

The year's record, held in the week's quiet, is the record that the year's events have produced, and the record's final reading is the one that the turning will conduct: the conflicts and the settlements, the decisions and the deferrals, the records and the rituals that the seasons have assembled. The reading is the year's conclusion, and the conclusion's form will be the new year's beginning; the record is waiting, in the last quiet, for the turning that the new year will bring.

The week's own character is the part of the days that the calendars cannot carry, the particular suspension that the year's end reserves for itself. The schedules that the year has kept are cleared for the turning, the messages that the offices exchange carry the seasonal greetings rather than the deadlines, and the newspapers that the week publishes review the year that has passed rather than the one that is coming; the suspension is the threshold's atmosphere, and the atmosphere is the week's particular gift. The year's habits are held in abeyance, and the holding is the quiet's own content.

The new year's arrival, as the week's end approaches, will be the turning that the year has been waiting for: the calendars will turn, the year's record will close, and the new year will begin with the agenda that the year's events have set. The days between arrived with the particular quiet that the calendar reserves, and the quiet is the year's last; the business is paused, the record is waiting, and the threshold is being kept with the particular calm that the turning's approach provides, which is the way that the years have always turned.
