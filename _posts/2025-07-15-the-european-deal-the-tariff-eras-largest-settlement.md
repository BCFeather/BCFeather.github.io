The European Union and the United States announced, in the middle of July, the agreement that the summer's negotiations had been building toward, and the announcement carried the year's largest trade settlement. The deal reduced the tariffs on the major sectors of the transatlantic trade, secured the access that the industries on both sides had pressed for, and established the mechanisms that the disputes will be settled through; the announcement, delivered through the joint statements that the negotiations had prepared, was the product of the decisive weeks that the summer had scheduled. The deal was the tariff era's proof of concept, and the proof's consequences will shape the year's remaining trade politics.

The deal's terms are the part that the analysts have weighed against the year's tariff arithmetic. The tariffs on the automobiles, the machinery and the agricultural products were reduced to the levels that the negotiators had mapped, the services and the regulatory cooperation were advanced through the mechanisms that the deal establishes, and the carve-outs that the two sides' politics required were written into the texts with the particular care that the sensitive sectors demand; the terms are the compromise that the two positions produced, and the compromise is the reason the deal could be announced. The deal's economics are real, and the reality will be measured in the trade flows that the coming quarters will record.

The politics of the deal are the part that the two governments are celebrating with the particular care that the year's audiences require. The American administration presents the deal as the demonstration of the doctrine, the proof that the tariffs extract the agreements that the old system could not produce; the European institutions present the deal as the management of the relationship with the largest trading partner, the securing of the access that the member states' economies need; the two presentations are compatible, and the compatibility is the deal's political value. The celebrations will be followed by the ratifications and the implementations, and the followings will test the deal's durability.

The deal's template effect is the part that the year's remaining negotiations will now be conducted under. The partners whose talks remain open, the Asian economies, the neighbours, the Gulf states, will read the European agreement for the signals about the terms that the administration will accept, and the reading will shape their own negotiations; the deal's carve-outs and its mechanisms will provide the reference points, and the reference points will be the starting positions of the talks that the year's second half will conduct. The European deal is the tariff era's largest settlement, and the settlement's shape will be the template that the remaining settlements will follow.

The deal's meaning, in the year's larger story, is the confirmation that the tariff era can produce the agreements that its doctrine promises. The threats have been applied, the costs have been measured, and the settlements have begun to arrive; the European deal is the largest of the settlements so far, and its arrival has changed the expectations of the markets, the industries and the governments that the year's trade politics touches. The deal will be implemented through the summer, the ratifications will be conducted, and the trade flows will be measured; the consequences will be the year's trade story, and the story's next chapters will be written by the negotiations that the European template will guide.
