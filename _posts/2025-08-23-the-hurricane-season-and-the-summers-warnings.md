The Atlantic's hurricane season moved through its peak weeks in late August, and the season's activity carried the warnings that the forecasts had issued through the summer: the storms forming in the waters that the season's warmth has made ready, the coasts preparing for the landfalls that the projections have mapped, and the year's climate extremes adding to the pressure on the emergency systems that the region has built. The season is the annual test of the region's preparedness, and the test has been conducted against the backdrop of the year's heat, the records that the summer has set, and the warnings that the scientists have repeated.

The season's activity, as the peak weeks arrived, was the activity that the forecasts had anticipated. The storms have formed in the numbers that the outlooks predicted, the strongest of them have intensified over the warm waters with the speed that the recent seasons have shown, and the projections have put the coasts on the alert through the weeks of the peak; the activity is the season's normal business, and the normalcy has been weighed against the warmth that the climate record has documented. The season's storms are the annual demonstration of the region's exposure, and the demonstration has been conducted with the particular attention that the year's extremes have earned.

The preparedness of the region is the part that the season tests with the regularity of the calendar. The emergency agencies have rehearsed the responses that the landfalls require, the evacuation plans have been updated with the lessons of the recent storms, and the communities have been reminded, through the warnings, of the procedures that the season demands; the preparedness is the infrastructure that the region has built through the years of the storms, and the infrastructure is being tested by the season's peak. The tests will be conducted storm by storm, and the conduct will be the season's real work.

The year's broader climate context is the part that the season cannot be separated from. The heat that the summer has recorded, the fires that the year has seen, and the warnings that the scientists have issued have all been present in the coverage that the season has generated, and the presence is the recognition that the storms are part of the larger pattern; the recognition is the scientific consensus, and the consensus has been translated, through the season's coverage, into the language that the region's planning requires. The climate context is the season's background, and the background's weight has been growing with the records.

The season's outcome will be measured through the autumn, when the peak passes and the storms' toll is counted; the landfalls, the damage, the lives, the recovery, all of it will be the season's record, and the record will be weighed against the forecasts and the preparations. The hurricane season moved through its peak weeks with the warnings that the forecasts had issued, and the warnings' fulfilment will be determined by the storms' course; the coasts prepared, the agencies rehearsed, and the season's business proceeded with the particular attention that the year's climate has earned. The season is the annual test, and the test's results will be written by the storms and the responses, in the late summer and the autumn that the forecasts will continue to watch.
